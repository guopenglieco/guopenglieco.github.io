A curated reading list of recent working papers and preprints on **network games**: strategic choices whose payoffs depend on interactions through a network.

<div class="watch-intro">
  <p><strong>Last reviewed: <time datetime="2026-11-16">16 November 2026</time></strong> &middot; 11 papers</p>
  <p>This edition covers papers first posted or revised between 16 May and 16 November 2026, ordered by their latest source date. Older first drafts are included when a revision falls within this window. Dates refer to repository postings and revisions.</p>
  <p>The focus is equilibrium, learning, and intervention in network games. Papers identified as already published in journals or conference proceedings are excluded. The notes below are brief summaries, not the authors’ abstracts.</p>
</div>

<ol class="network-watch">
  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2611-13715">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13715">Optimal Regulation and Information Institutions with Hidden Network Equilibria</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Christian Jaag</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v1<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-11-10">10 November 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Information and regulation</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Studies a regulator choosing both an activity charge and an information institution in a two-population network game. Aggregate behavior can hide distinct specialization equilibria. Comparing monitoring, certification, and audited reporting, the paper shows how information costs change targeted regulation, under explicit assumptions about verification and the available institutions.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13715">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13715v1">PDF (v1)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2610-42087">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42087">From Scalar Games to Unconstrained Network Zero-Sum Games: Transfer Principles for Gradient-Based Learning</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Gaoqi Xue and James P. Bailey</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v1<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-10-30">30 October 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Learning in network games</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> For a class of gradient-based learning rules, convergence guarantees for scalar zero-sum games transfer to unconstrained network zero-sum games. A decomposition of the network game operator also transfers finite-time bounds, connecting allowable step sizes and worst-case learning rates to the corresponding scalar problems.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42087">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42087v1">PDF (v1)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2610-33230">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33230">Targeting Interventions for Volatility Reduction in Network Systems - Part I: Idiosyncratic Shocks</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Luca Damonte, Leonardo Cianfanelli, Giacomo Como, and Fabio Fagnani</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v1<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-10-23">23 October 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Network interventions and shocks</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Analyzes interventions that reduce aggregate volatility in linear network equilibrium models, including network games. A centrality measure identifies how node-level shocks are amplified. Optimal spending depends jointly on centrality, shock variance, and intervention costs; a larger budget expands the set of targeted nodes. The paper also considers uncertainty about shock variances.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33230">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33230v1">PDF (v1)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="ssrn-7487222">
    <h2><a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7487222">A Katz-Bonacich envelope for network games with large spillovers</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Zhigang Cao, Sijie Wang, and Feng Zhu</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">SSRN working paper<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-09-21">21 September 2026</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-09-24">24 September 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Strategic complementarities and centrality</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Extends centrality-based equilibrium characterization to linear network games with large spillovers, bounded actions, and strictly positive autarkic optima. The equilibrium is the componentwise lower envelope of auxiliary games satisfying the small-spillover condition. This representation retains a Katz-Bonacich interpretation while making the relevant subnetwork endogenous.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7487222">SSRN record</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2607-10997">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10997">Equilibria in heterogeneous multi-strategy network games</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Shan Pei, Wenjie Cao, and Boyu Zhang</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v2<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-07-13">13 July 2026</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-09-20">20 September 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Heterogeneous players and equilibrium</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Studies multi-strategy games with conformists, rebels, and stubborn players. Equilibrium existence depends on network architecture and how player types are connected. The paper gives sufficient conditions on general networks, sharper characterizations or algorithms for several familiar architectures, and nonexistence results for specified classes of large random networks.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10997">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10997v2">PDF (v2)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="ssrn-6819358">
    <h2><a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6819358">Learning Networks</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Promit K. Chaudhuri, Jonghyun Park, Sudipta Sarangi, and Hector Tzavellas</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">SSRN working paper<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-05-26">26 May 2026</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-09-07">7 September 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Learning under network uncertainty</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Studies players who observe local neighborhoods and learn about the wider network from repeated actions. Learning ends in finite time, after which play matches the complete-information Nash equilibrium set even when the network remains unidentified. The paper links identification to heterogeneous equilibrium behavior and, in linear-quadratic games, to Katz-Bonacich centralities.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6819358">SSRN record</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2602-12897">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12897">Network Interventions: Targeting Agents or Targeting Links?</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Krishna Dasaratha and Anant Shah</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v2<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-02-13">13 February 2026</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-09-05">5 September 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Network interventions and endogenous links</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Studies subsidies in a network game where agents choose both actions and links. When links have nonnegative intrinsic value, subsidizing actions alone is optimal. When that intrinsic value is negative, the paper identifies conditions requiring link subsidies, showing why endogenous connections change the design of network interventions.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12897">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12897v2">PDF (v2)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2607-16036">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16036">Network-Induced Strategic Communication in Opinion Dynamics</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Hassan Munif, Anthony Couthures, Vineeth S. Varma, Samson Lasaulce, and Tamer Başar</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v1<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-07-17">17 July 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Strategic communication on networks</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Derives communication rules from a sender-receiver game on a weighted influence network. A network-dependent exaggeration factor links the communication problem to scalar cheap-talk games. Depending on incentives and receiver beliefs, equilibrium communication takes the form of averaging, saturated signals, or interval quantization, which then shapes repeated opinion dynamics.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16036">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16036v1">PDF (v1)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2607-05932">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05932">Network games with heterogeneous players</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Wenjie Cao, Angel Sanchez, and Boyu Zhang</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v1<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-07-07">7 July 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Heterogeneous players and dynamics</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Develops a framework for binary-action network games with heterogeneous payoffs, using an equivalent representation with conformist, rebel, and stubborn player types. It studies pure-equilibrium existence and best-response dynamics, and connects equilibrium behavior to the mixing of player types. Extensions address limited information and applications to real social networks.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05932">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05932v1">PDF (v1)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2602-16071">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16071">A Theory of Network Games Part 1: Utility Representations</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Joseph Root and Evan Sadler</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v3<br>First posted: <time datetime="2026-02-17">17 February 2026</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-06-05">5 June 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Foundations of network games</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Provides axiomatic foundations for utility specifications used in network games. Bilateral strategic interactions correspond to additive separability across opponents, while additional restrictions yield utilities linear in opponents’ actions. Linear best replies together with midpoint indifference characterize the familiar linear-quadratic specification, clarifying which behavioral assumptions support common models.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16071">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16071v3">PDF (v3)</a></p>
  </li>

  <li class="watch-paper" id="arxiv-2505-09799">
    <h2><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09799">On Signed Network Games with Binary Actions</a></h2>
    <p class="watch-authors">Martina Vanelli, Laura Arditti, Giacomo Como, and Fabio Fagnani</p>
    <p class="watch-meta">arXiv preprint &middot; v3<br>First posted: <time datetime="2025-05-14">14 May 2025</time> &middot; Revised: <time datetime="2026-06-01">1 June 2026</time></p>
    <p class="watch-topic">Signed networks and equilibrium</p>
    <p class="watch-summary"><strong>Summary.</strong> Studies binary-action games on directed signed networks, allowing strategic complements and substitutes in the same model. Under conditions on a cohesive or structurally balanced group and the remaining players, equilibria exhibit consensus or polarization within that group. The analysis also establishes stability under best-response transitions.</p>
    <p class="watch-links"><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09799">arXiv record</a> &middot; <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09799v3">PDF (v3)</a></p>
  </li>
